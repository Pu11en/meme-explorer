# Free Solana chain and RPC paths — independent audit

**Snapshot:** 2026-08-26 UTC  
**Rule:** “Free” excludes a funded wallet, prepaid/metered balance, paid package, card requirement, and temporary trial. Primary/owner sources only; no undocumented frontend routes or scraping. Observed endpoint behavior is evidence of current availability, not an SLA.

## Corrected findings

### Protocol and Solana-operated public RPC

- A workable raw-chain flow is `logsSubscribe` with one `mentions` pubkey, followed by `getTransaction` and Pump IDL decoding. `programSubscribe` reports changes to accounts owned by a program; `accountSubscribe` reports changes to one known account. These are protocol capabilities, not delivery or completeness guarantees. `getTransaction` metadata can include errors, logs, inner instructions, and pre/post token balances, but RPC JSON fields can be `null` or omitted when the validator did not record them.
- Commitment is a confidence/latency tradeoff. Detect at `processed` or `confirmed`, but reconcile canonical records at `finalized`; no subscription implies replay after a disconnect.
- The current clusters page names `https://api.mainnet.solana.com`. The legacy/mainnet-beta alias `https://api.mainnet-beta.solana.com` and its WSS form responded during this audit, but observed alias availability is not a contractual endpoint promise.
- Solana says its public endpoints are rate-limited, may change, and are unsuitable for production. Owner documentation publishes 100 requests/10 seconds/IP, 40 requests/10 seconds/IP for one RPC method, 40 concurrent connections, 40 connection attempts/10 seconds, and 100 MB/30 seconds. Runtime headers on 2026-08-26 instead advertised 250 RPS, a 150-method limit, 40 connections, 40 connection attempts, and 10 PubSub subscriptions. Treat headers as transient runtime policy, not an SLA.
- Safe probes succeeded for `getHealth`, `getFirstAvailableBlock`, `minimumLedgerSlot`, `getTokenSupply`, a Pump `logsSubscribe`, and `getTransaction`. Two attempts to call `getTokenLargestAccounts` returned HTTP 429 with `x-ratelimit-method-limit: 0`; therefore protocol support does not establish current usability on the free public endpoint.
- `getFirstAvailableBlock` and `minimumLedgerSlot` do not mean the same thing: the former reports the lowest confirmed block for which the responding node says it has ledger information, while the latter is the first slot retained in the node’s local ledger. During the probe they returned `0` and `441759842` respectively. Neither proves complete transaction retrieval or a free archive retention SLA.
- Historical traversal is explicit and bounded: `getSignaturesForAddress` returns at most 1,000 rows per call and needs `before` pagination; `getTransaction` may still return `null`.
- `getTokenSupply` and `getTokenLargestAccounts` return a context slot and accept commitment; the latter is only the 20 largest token accounts and is not a holder/beneficial-owner distribution. Persist exact `amount`/`uiAmountString`, not deprecated floating `uiAmount`.

Primary Solana sources: [clusters/public endpoints](https://solana.com/docs/references/clusters), [RPC commitment](https://solana.com/docs/rpc), [`logsSubscribe`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/websocket/logssubscribe), [`programSubscribe`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/websocket/programsubscribe), [`accountSubscribe`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/websocket/accountsubscribe), [`getTransaction`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/gettransaction), [RPC JSON structures](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/json-structures), [`getFirstAvailableBlock`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/getfirstavailableblock), [`minimumLedgerSlot`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/minimumledgerslot), [`getTokenSupply`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/gettokensupply), [`getTokenLargestAccounts`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/gettokenlargestaccounts).

### Hosted plans that remain relevant under the free-only rule

- **Alchemy:** owner pricing lists an ongoing Free tier with 30M CUs/month, 300 CUs/s base throughput, Smart WebSockets, Node API, Full Archive Data, email support, and no custom SLA. Solana HTTPS/WSS methods are documented. This is the strongest documented free WSS + archive classification, but “Full Archive Data” is not a stated retention duration or reliability promise. Alchemy Yellowstone gRPC requires PAYG or Enterprise, so it is not free. Sources: [pricing plans](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/reference/pricing-plans), [Solana RPC](https://www.alchemy.com/rpc/solana), [Solana subscriptions](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/reference/solana-subscription-api-endpoints), [Yellowstone quickstart](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/reference/yellowstone-grpc-quickstart).
- **Chainstack:** the owner pricing page lists Developer at $0, no card required, 3M request units/month, one node, WebSockets, community support, and a displayed 25 requests/s benefit. Archive data starts with paid Growth. However, Chainstack’s separate throughput guide says Developer is 5 RPS. Design to 5 RPS until a dashboard/contract test proves the higher limit. Sources: [pricing](https://chainstack.com/pricing/), [throughput guidelines](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/limits), [archive data](https://chainstack.com/archive-data/).
- **Ankr Freemium:** owner docs say sign-in without topping up yields 200M API credits/month, no balance to manage, HTTPS only, Public rate limits (about 1,800 requests/minute across endpoints), limited priority during high traffic, community support, and no contractual SLA. The generic plan table says “Full and Archive data,” but the Solana-specific owner page says its Solana nodes retain only about 16 hours and older `getTransaction`/`getSignaturesForAddress` data can return `null`; therefore do **not** treat free Ankr Solana as archive. A no-key call to `https://rpc.ankr.com/solana` returned HTTP 403 (“API key is not allowed”), so only the signed-in Freemium key path is supported by current evidence; the generic Public/no-key Solana claim is not currently observed. Sources: [plans](https://www.ankr.com/docs/rpc-service/service-plans/), [charging policy](https://www.ankr.com/docs/rpc-service/charging-policy/), [SLA](https://www.ankr.com/docs/rpc-service/sla/), [Solana chain notes](https://www.ankr.com/docs/rpc-service/chains/chains-api/solana/).
- **Helius:** owner docs list Free at $0, 1M credits/month, 10 RPC requests/s, standard Solana LaserStream WSS, and community support. Helius extensions are not on Free; mainnet LaserStream gRPC requires Business/Professional. Helius itself says Free is suitable for development/testing and recommends paid plans for production. The reviewed owner pages do not explicitly prove that initial dashboard enrollment never asks for payment details, so verify onboarding before counting it under the strict no-card rule. Sources: [plans](https://www.helius.dev/docs/billing/plans), [billing FAQ](https://www.helius.dev/docs/faqs/billing).
- **Shyft:** the owner pricing page advertises Free at $0 with unlimited credits, 10 RPC requests/s, 1 API request/s, and no gRPC. This supports a candidate free HTTP-RPC path, not free Yellowstone. The public pricing material reviewed did not explicitly prove no-card onboarding or publish an SLA; verify signup before strict inclusion. Source: [Shyft RPC/gRPC pricing](https://shyft.to/solana-rpc-grpc-pricing).

### Excluded paths

- **QuickNode:** current pricing is a one-month free trial (10M credits, no overage), not an ongoing free entitlement. Exclude it from a durable free-only prototype even though Solana HTTPS/WSS and archive are trial features. [Pricing](https://www.quicknode.com/pricing), [Solana endpoints](https://www.quicknode.com/docs/solana/endpoints).
- **Mainnet gRPC:** no surveyed owner source establishes an ongoing free mainnet Yellowstone path. Helius requires Business/Professional; Alchemy requires PAYG/Enterprise; Shyft Free says no gRPC; QuickNode includes it on paid Scale/Business; Chainstack’s Geyser stream starts at $49; Triton requires a $125 deposit. [Helius plans](https://www.helius.dev/docs/billing/plans), [Alchemy Yellowstone](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/reference/yellowstone-grpc-quickstart), [Shyft pricing](https://shyft.to/solana-rpc-grpc-pricing), [QuickNode gRPC](https://www.quicknode.com/docs/solana/solana-grpc/overview), [Chainstack changelog](https://docs.chainstack.com/changelog/chainstack-updates-september-30-2025), [Triton pricing](https://www.triton.one/pricing/).
- **Protocol support ⇒ production readiness:** rejected. Every free path reviewed lacks a production SLA or is expressly discouraged for production; availability must be measured and failures backfilled.

## Prototype recommendation

Use **Alchemy Free** as the primary documented WSS/archive-classified source after confirming no-card signup, and **Chainstack Developer** as the explicitly no-card secondary live source. Keep the Solana-operated public RPC as a zero-key diagnostic/emergency source, not a production dependency. If Alchemy onboarding violates the strict rule, use Chainstack plus signed-in Ankr Freemium/Helius/Shyft only after their signup flows pass the no-card test. No free combination removes the need for reconnect gap detection, HTTP backfill, finalized reconciliation, and local raw-event storage.

## Required tests before calling the prototype production-capable

1. Run two independent WSS feeds for at least 24 hours; compare signatures, disconnects, duplicates, and time to confirmed/finalized.
2. On reconnect, backfill from the last finalized slot/signature over HTTP; do not assume replay.
3. For each signature, retry `getTransaction` with `maxSupportedTransactionVersion`; persist raw response, slot, commitment, local receive time, error, logs, inner instructions, and token balances.
4. Probe several known-old Pump signatures per provider and record `getFirstAvailableBlock`, `minimumLedgerSlot`, and null/error behavior monthly.
5. Contract-test expensive/restricted methods (`getTokenLargestAccounts`, `getProgramAccounts`) because protocol documentation does not guarantee free endpoint enablement.
6. Record HTTP 429/403, WSS silence, and null history as explicit missingness; use exponential backoff, circuit breaking, and provider failover.
